Where is the E. coli coming from?

Station NBC-01, Nereid Boat Club dock, Lower Passaic River. This page lines up the 2026 sampling season against rainfall, tide and the mapped sewer and stormwater outfalls, to narrow down likely sources.

1. Rain and tide heatmap

Each cell is the geometric mean E. coli for samples taken under that combination of 48-hour rainfall and tide direction. Darker means more bacteria.

  • High on a falling tide after rain: points upstream (combined sewers above the dock, tributaries, storm drains).
  • High on a rising tide: points downstream, with the tide carrying overflow water back up the river.
  • High with no rain: points to a steady source such as a leaking sewer or an illicit connection.

What this can and cannot show

  • One station and eleven visits can rank explanations. They cannot prove a source.
  • Most heatmap cells hold one or two samples. Treat each cell as a hint.
  • The dock position is published to two decimal places, so distances are approximate (about half a mile).
  • Harbor Dischargers Group results in the federal portal are historical and use a certified lab method, so they are context, not a same-day comparison.
  • The next step that would localize a source is same-day sampling at several points above and below the suspected outfalls after rain.
